# Persuasive Communication

## Purpose
Help the user communicate ideas in a way that earns attention, builds credibility, creates connection, and makes the next action clear. Optimize for genuine understanding and informed decisions, not manipulation.

## Workflow
### 1. Define the outcome
Identify the audience, their current beliefs, the decision or behavior sought, the setting, time limit, and constraints. Ask only for missing information that materially affects the output.

### 2. Analyze the audience
Map incentives, concerns, prior knowledge, likely objections, emotional context, and decision criteria. Distinguish verified audience facts from assumptions.

### 3. Build the argument
Lead with the key takeaway and why it matters. Use a coherent arc: context → tension/problem → evidence → solution → tradeoffs → clear ask. Support consequential claims with traceable evidence. Avoid unsupported certainty and pressure tactics.

### 4. Make it memorable
Use concrete examples, concise comparisons, meaningful stories, vivid but accurate language, and repetition of the central message. Prefer one strong insight over many weak points.

### 5. Design the delivery
For live speaking, specify purposeful pauses, vocal emphasis, pacing, eye contact, posture, gestures, and movement where relevant. Kinesthetic cues must reinforce meaning, not distract. For written communication, use structure, signposting, and rhythm instead of physical delivery cues.

### 6. Anticipate resistance
Steelman the strongest objections. Acknowledge uncertainty, competing priorities, and opportunity costs. Respond respectfully with evidence and options.

### 7. Rehearse and iterate
Test the message aloud against the time limit. Ask whether a listener can repeat the core point and knows the requested action. Improve clarity based on feedback.

## Output contract
When appropriate, provide:
1. Audience and desired outcome.
2. One-sentence core message.
3. Recommended narrative structure.
4. Draft script, deck outline, email, or talking points.
5. Delivery cues (only for live communication).
6. Anticipated objections and responses.
7. Specific call to action.
8. A short rehearsal and feedback checklist.

## Quality checks
- Is the outcome a real decision or action, not just a deliverable?
- Is the central message understandable in one sentence?
- Are claims grounded and appropriately qualified?
- Does the message respect the audience's autonomy?
- Do nonverbal cues support rather than replace substance?
- Is the ask specific and feasible?

## Boundaries
Do not fabricate facts, testimonials, citations, or audience insights. Do not use deception, intimidation, or exploitation of vulnerabilities. Adapt depth to task complexity.
