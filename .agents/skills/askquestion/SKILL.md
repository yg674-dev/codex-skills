# AskQuestion — Interview Closing Questions

## Principle
Do not merely ask what the job entails; ask how the candidate can help solve the team's problems. Treat the closing questions as a two-way evaluation of fit, not a trick that guarantees an offer.

## Workflow
1. Identify the company, role, job description, interviewer role (recruiter, hiring manager, peer, executive), interview stage, and time remaining. Use known context; ask only for critical missing details.
2. Infer 2–3 likely team challenges from the JD and verified company information, marking inferences as hypotheses.
3. Choose **one to three** questions based on the interviewer and conversational flow, not a fixed script. Avoid repeating what the interviewer has already answered.
4. Write natural, concise English questions with optional Chinese translations, and a one-line reason each question matters.
5. Add a follow-up question that responds to the interviewer's actual answer, and optionally a 15–25-second closing statement linking the candidate's relevant evidence to the need.
6. For interview practice, role-play plausible answers and coach the user's response without inventing company facts.

## Three core questions

### 1. What do you need solved?
**English:** "If I joined the team, what would be the most important problem you'd want me to help solve in the first 90 days? And where could I take work off the team's plate in the first month?"

**中文：**“如果我有机会加入，您最希望我在前三个月解决什么核心问题？第一个月我能在哪些方面帮团队分担？”

Best for: hiring manager, team lead, business owner.

Listen for: urgent delivery needs, dependencies, unclear ownership, onboarding support, and measurable outcomes. Follow up: "How would you measure success on that?"

### 2. What does excellence look like?
**English:** "What are one or two qualities that distinguish someone who excels in this role?" 

Alternative: "What would exceeding expectations in the first six months look like?"

For an experienced business leader: "For someone hoping to grow in this field, what's one piece of advice you'd prioritize?"

Best for: hiring manager, senior peer, business leader.

Listen for: execution, judgment, collaboration, technical depth, customer empathy, and performance expectations. Use the answer to evaluate fit, not to reflexively flatter.

### 3. What should I prepare before joining?
**English:** "Based on our conversation, if I had the opportunity to join, what would you recommend I learn or prepare beforehand so I could contribute quickly?"

**中文：**“结合今天的交流，如果有机会加入，您建议我提前学习或准备什么，才能更快为团队创造价值？”

Best for: hiring manager or future teammate; often a strong final question.

If they identify a gap, briefly acknowledge it and offer **specific existing evidence** or a realistic learning plan. Do not argue or overclaim.

## Selection by interviewer
- **Recruiter:** Ask about interview process, evaluation criteria, team structure, and role priorities; compensation, benefits, work authorization, and schedule are legitimate questions when relevant.
- **Hiring manager:** Prioritize the 90-day problem and success criteria.
- **Future teammate:** Ask about collaboration friction, handoffs, and what would make their work easier.
- **Executive:** Ask about strategic bets, customer adoption barriers, and the highest-leverage business problem.

## Output format
- Interview context (known facts versus assumptions)
- Top 3 recommended questions, ranked by fit
- Best one if time is limited
- Optional follow-up tailored to the likely answer
- Natural spoken English phrasing (Chinese translation if useful)
- Closing line connecting the candidate's relevant experience to the team's challenge

## Guardrails
- Never claim these questions guarantee an offer or reveal the interviewer's true hiring decision.
- Do not forbid questions about compensation, benefits, workload, work authorization, or culture; these can be important to the candidate and are often best directed to HR.
- Avoid generic flattery, manipulative presuppositions, or excessive rehearsed wording.
- Do not assume every JD is comprehensive; asking for clarification about day-to-day responsibilities is valid when needed.
- Never invent personal achievements or company information.
- Prefer genuine curiosity and mutual fit over 'passing' an interview at any cost.
